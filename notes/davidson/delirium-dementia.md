## Delirium
- Term replaces "acute confusional state" (p1138). A medical disorder, common in older adults, HDU and ICU (p1240).
- About 25% of all hospital inpatients, 50% after hip fracture, 75% in ICU (p1304).
- Acute change (hours to days) that fluctuates; the defining feature is a deficit in **attention** (p1304).
- Subtypes: hyperactive (agitated), hypoactive (lethargic, sedated), mixed. Hypoactive is more common in frailty
  and easily missed (p1304); in ICU hypoactive is far more common (p213).
- Impaired consciousness and loss of orientation, typically **visual** hallucinations; visual/taste/smell
  hallucinations suggest an organic cause, auditory suggest schizophrenia (p1241, p1252).
- Most common cause of disturbed behaviour in old age (Box 31.6, p1240).
- In frailty, infection may present only as delirium (p1302); in old age all drugs can cause delirium (p170).
- Risk factors: frailty, dementia, previous delirium (p1304). Powerful risk factor for later dementia;
  "persistent delirium" overlaps with dementia (p1304).
- Diagnosis: 4AT (alertness, AMT4, months backwards, acute change/fluctuation); score ≥4 = possible delirium (Box 34.11).
- Assessment: identify precipitants, especially acute illness; drug and alcohol history (recently started/stopped);
  neuro signs or head injury → imaging (p1304).
- Management (p1304–1305): prevention is best (HELP programme; Box 34.10: treat constipation, avoid catheters,
  fluids/food, orientation, limit ward moves, medication review, sleep, glasses/hearing aids). Treat precipitants;
  life-threatening first: sepsis, drug/alcohol intoxication or withdrawal, hypoglycaemia, other metabolic. Trivial
  factors (constipation, catheter) can cause or prolong it in frailty. Non-drug strategies first line.
- Drugs: low-dose antipsychotic only with psychotic features plus extreme distress or safety risk (p1305).
  In ICU, olanzapine/quetiapine may work better than haloperidol; drugs do not help as prophylaxis or in
  hypoactive delirium (p213).
- Sedation cautions (p1245): benzodiazepines → respiratory depression (lung disease), encephalopathy (liver
  disease); antipsychotics → acute dystonia, arrhythmias (heart disease). Procyclidine for extrapyramidal effects;
  flumazenil reverses benzodiazepine respiratory depression.
- Alcohol: delirium tremens peaks 2–3 days after last drink (agitation, visual hallucinations, tremor, sweating,
  tachycardia; significant mortality) (p1249). Treat withdrawal with long-acting benzodiazepines, tailed over 5–7
  days (p1250). Wernicke (nystagmus/ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, delirium) from thiamine deficiency (p1249); give
  thiamine before glucose if suspected (p1138).
- Other causes named in the book: opioid toxicity (p168), hyponatraemia, hypercalcaemia, adrenal insufficiency in
  old age, anticholinergics and amantadine (p1167), serotonin syndrome, NMS, non-convulsive status in old age (p1158).

## Dementia
- Loss of previously acquired intellectual function **without impaired arousal**; global, typically progressive
  and non-reversible. 5% over 65, 20% over 85. Commonest: Alzheimer's and diffuse vascular (p1246).
- Mild cognitive impairment = cognitive impairment without functional limitation (p1304).
- History from patient and informant; MoCA bedside test; look for depression (p1246). Depression can present as
  "pseudo-dementia", often reversible with antidepressants (p1138).
- Course: gradual = Alzheimer's; stepwise = vascular; Lewy body = visual hallucinations, fluctuating cognition;
  FTD = frontal/temporal signs, memory relatively preserved early; CJD = rapid (months) with myoclonus (p1246–1249).
- Investigations aim to find treatable causes (Box 31.21): CT/MRI; FBC, ESR, U&E, glucose, calcium, LFT, TFT,
  B12, syphilis serology, ANA/anti-dsDNA; CXR; EEG; selected: LP, HIV, brain biopsy (p1247).
- Treatable/less common causes (Box 31.20): hydrocephalus, chronic subdural, tumour, alcohol, thiamine and B12
  deficiency, syphilis, HIV, etc. Alcoholic dementia does not progress and may improve with abstinence (p1250).
- Management (p1247): treat causes, support patients and carers; risk factors (BP in vascular; abstinence and
  vitamins in toxic/nutritional). Psychotropics with care: increased mortality with long-term use; sedation is
  not a substitute for good care. Capacity / mental health legislation for affairs and placement.
- Alzheimer's (p1248): anticholinesterases (donepezil, rivastigmine, galantamine) slow progression in early
  stages; memantine (NMDA antagonist) in early disease and selected advanced cases; antidepressants if depressed.
  ApoE4 testing not clinically useful (p1247).
- FTD: no specific treatment; SSRIs for disinhibition; anticholinesterases do not work (p1249).
- Lewy body: dementia plus parkinsonism; very sensitive to antipsychotics and anti-Parkinsonian drugs;
  anticholinesterases can help (p1249). Rivastigmine licensed in Parkinson's disease dementia (UK) (p1167).
