# F10–F19 Disorders due to psychoactive substances: notes from Davidson 24th ed (2022)

Paraphrased, with printed page numbers. Re-check against the book before publishing.

- Box 31.1 (p1236): block F10–F19 "Disorders due to psychoactive substances: alcohol, opioids, cannabinoids
  etc."; examples: intoxication, harmful use, dependence, withdrawal.
- Box 31.7 (p1240): extra problems of substance misuse. Route: IV (abscess, cellulitis, thrombosis;
  endocarditis, hepatitis, HIV), nasal (septal erosion, epistaxis), inhalation (burns, chemical pneumonitis,
  rashes), smoking (oral, laryngeal, lung cancer). Pressure to prescribe: manipulation, deceit, threats,
  factitious illness, malingering.

## Alcohol (p1240, p1249–1250)
- Misuse = drinking with social, psychological or physical problems; dependence is narrower (Box 31.22:
  narrowed repertoire, salience, tolerance, repeated withdrawal, relief drinking, compulsion, reinstatement).
- About a quarter of male medical inpatients in the UK have a current or past alcohol problem.
- Acute intoxication: ataxia, slurred speech, emotional incontinence, aggression; hypoglycaemia,
  ketoacidosis, aspiration, respiratory depression; accidents (Box 31.23).
- Withdrawal syndrome peaks 2–3 days after the last drink, can include seizures; withdrawal phenomena:
  restlessness, anxiety, panic; tachycardia, sweating, mydriasis, nausea, vomiting; delirium (classically
  Lilliputian hallucinations); seizures.
- Delirium tremens: severe withdrawal = delirium (agitation, visual hallucinations) + hyperarousal (tremor,
  sweating, tachycardia); significant mortality.
- Alcoholic hallucinosis: auditory hallucinations in clear consciousness, not in withdrawal.
- Alcoholic blackouts: amnesia for periods of intoxication.
- Wernicke–Korsakoff (thiamin deficiency): Wernicke = nystagmus/ophthalmoplegia, ataxia, delirium (acute,
  can become irreversible); Korsakoff = severe short-term memory loss with confabulation.
- Alcohol-related brain damage (ARBD); alcoholic dementia (does not progress, may improve with abstinence).
- Psychiatric: low mood/depression, suicide, anxiety. Social: absenteeism, unemployment, marital, child abuse,
  financial, legal problems. Organ damage: liver, GI, pancreas, heart, nerves, etc. (Box 31.23).
- Tests: raised MCV and/or GGT support it but are abnormal in only half of problem drinkers.

## Other drugs (p1250–1251)
- Sedatives (physical dependence: tolerance + withdrawal): benzodiazepines, opiates (morphine, heroin,
  methadone, dihydrocodeine), barbiturates. Overdose kills mainly by respiratory depression. Opiate
  withdrawal from ~12 h: craving, rhinorrhoea, lacrimation, yawning, sweating, shivering, piloerection,
  vomiting, diarrhoea, cramps; tachycardia, hypertension, mydriasis, flushing. Benzodiazepine/barbiturate
  withdrawal: prolonged anxiety, hallucinations, seizures. IV use: bacterial infections, hepatitis B/C, HIV.
- Stimulants (amphetamines, cocaine): no physical dependence, but withdrawal low mood and craving (crack
  cocaine); cardiac and cerebrovascular problems; chronic use: paranoid psychosis like schizophrenia; high
  doses: toxic psychosis (delirium); formication ("ants under the skin").
- Hallucinogens (cannabis, ecstasy, LSD, psilocybin, synthetic cannabinoids / novel psychoactive
  substances): toxic confusional state after heavy cannabis; acute psychosis; heavy cannabis use raises
  schizophrenia risk; paranoid psychosis with ecstasy; chronic psychosis after regular LSD.
- Organic solvents (glue sniffing): euphoria, excitement, dizziness, floating; loss of consciousness, death
  from toxicity or asphyxia.
- Polydrug misuse is common. Diagnosis: history, complications, urine/blood drug screen.
